What the tool cannot promise

A satellite greenness signal does not prove soil improvement. Regional rainfall or groundwater trends do not describe the water available to one field. Rotation outcomes also depend on cultivar, management, irrigation, pests, prices and weather. Treat every option as a question to investigate with local expertise.

A FEW SIMPLE DEFINITIONS

Understand the evidence.

The map and charts are clues to discuss with an agronomist, not the whole story of a farm.

NDVI

A satellite-based greenness indicator calculated from reflected red and near-infrared light. It can help track vegetation growth patterns, but it does not directly measure yield, soil fertility or soil organic matter.

Evapotranspiration (ET)

Water moving from the land to the air through evaporation from soil and water surfaces plus plant transpiration. It helps describe crop water use, but estimates need local weather and crop context.

Soil-moisture anomaly

How wet or dry a period is compared with the usual conditions for the same place and season. A satellite product represents an area around its footprint, not every part of a field.

Frost / heat risk

The chance that temperatures cross crop-sensitive thresholds during important growth stages. Risk depends on the crop, variety, growth stage, timing and local microclimate.

Nitrogen fixation

Some legumes work with soil bacteria to convert nitrogen gas from the air into forms that can enter the plant-soil system. The amount left for the next crop depends on biomass, harvest and management.

Satellite vs ground measurements

Satellites observe or estimate conditions over pixels and revisit at set intervals. Soil labs, rain gauges and field sensors measure specific sites. The strongest assessment combines both and records uncertainty.
